## The cost people forget to budget for

When clients come to Codexier AB asking about an AI feature, the first number they usually have in mind is what the model or API will cost to run. That part is often the cheapest line item. The expensive part is everything around it: collecting and cleaning the data the feature needs, handling the cases where the model gets it wrong, and reviewing outputs on an ongoing basis so mistakes do not quietly pile up. A quote that only covers the model is a quote that will surprise the client later.

## Where the real cost actually lives

- Data preparation, which almost always takes longer than expected, especially if the data was never collected with this use case in mind.
- Handling edge cases, like unusual customer inputs or rare situations the model was never shown during development.
- Ongoing monitoring, because a model that works well at launch can quietly get worse as real-world data shifts over time.
- Human review for anything customer-facing or high-stakes, since AI output usually still needs a person checking it, at least at first.

These costs do not show up in a demo. They show up months later, in maintenance.

## A simple way to estimate before committing

Before scoping any AI feature, I ask three questions: how clean is the existing data, what happens when the model is wrong, and who reviews the output ongoing. The answers usually reveal the real cost faster than any technical estimate. A feature with messy data, high-stakes mistakes, and no review plan is a much bigger project than the same feature with clean data, low-stakes mistakes, and a lightweight review process.

## What this means for planning

If you are budgeting for an AI feature, treat the model as a small fraction of the total cost, not the whole cost. The lasting expense is the system around the model: the data pipeline, the review process, and the maintenance that keeps it accurate over time. Planning for that upfront avoids the common trap of a feature that looks affordable at launch and expensive a year later.
