## There's no universal right number

People ask this question expecting a formula: three layers for this kind of problem, ten for that one. There isn't one. The right number of layers depends on how complex the relationship is between your inputs and outputs, how much data you have, and how much computing time you're willing to spend. What I can give you is a way to think about the decision instead of a number to copy.

## What extra layers actually buy you

Each additional layer gives the network more capacity to represent complex, abstract patterns. Early layers tend to pick up simple structure, and deeper layers combine that structure into more abstract representations. This is genuinely useful for hard problems, image recognition, language understanding, anything where the input is unstructured and the relationships are layered themselves.

But capacity is not free. More layers mean more parameters, which means you need more data to train them properly, more compute, and a higher risk of overfitting, where the model memorizes your training set instead of learning a pattern that generalizes.

## A practical way to decide

I start small on purpose. A shallow network, sometimes just one or two hidden layers, often performs better than expected on tasks that aren't deeply complex. I train it, check whether it's underfitting, meaning it can't even learn the training data well, and only then consider adding depth. If a small model can't capture the pattern even with enough training time, that's a real signal to add a layer and test again. If it's already overfitting, the fix is almost never more depth, it's more data, regularization, or a simpler model.

## What actually matters more than depth

In my experience, beginners obsess over layer count far more than the problem deserves. Data quality, the right features, sensible preprocessing, and proper validation usually move your results more than adding another hidden layer ever will. Depth is a tool for specific situations, not a proxy for how serious your project looks.
