## Stop planning, find one real client

If I could go back to before Codexier AB existed, the first thing I would say is to stop refining the plan and go find one real client willing to pay for something small. I spent more time than I should have designing services and pricing tiers for a company that had not done a single paid project yet. One actual client, even a small one, taught me more in two weeks about what people would pay for than months of planning did.

## Your first client is a data point, not a template

The first client rarely represents your eventual best client, and I made the mistake of shaping early decisions around them as if they did. Their specific requests felt like signals about what the market wanted, when really they were just one company's particular situation. I would tell myself to learn from the first few clients without immediately building permanent processes around their exact preferences.

## Underpricing costs more than it seems to

I priced early Codexier AB work low, telling myself it was fine because I was building a portfolio and relationships. What I did not account for is how hard it is to raise prices with an existing client later, and how underpricing attracts clients who specifically value low cost over the things I actually wanted to be known for. I would tell myself to price for the company I wanted to become, not the one trying to win its first deal at any cost.

## You will figure out the rest by doing it

The advice I needed most was simpler than any specific tactic: most of what matters about running a company cannot be learned before you start, only while you are doing it. I spent real time trying to feel ready before starting. Readiness came from doing the work, not from more preparation beforehand, and I would have started months earlier if I had believed that from the start.
