## It was never the code

Before founding Codexier AB, I assumed the hardest part would be technical: building reliable systems, picking the right architecture, getting AI features to behave consistently. Those problems are real, but they are solvable. Given enough focused time, a hard technical problem gets fixed. The harder problem was never in the code editor.

## Trust takes months, doubt takes a sentence

The hardest part has been earning and keeping client trust. A single missed deadline or one feature that did not behave as promised can undo months of good work in a client's mind, even if everything else has gone well. Rebuilding that confidence takes far longer than losing it did. I underestimated how much of running a company is just consistently being the kind of partner people do not have to worry about.

## Saying no is harder than saying yes

Early on, I said yes to almost any project that came in, because turning down revenue felt irresponsible for a young company. That habit nearly cost more than it earned. Projects that did not fit Codexier AB's actual strengths drained time that should have gone to clients we were genuinely good for. Learning to say no, clearly and early, to work that was not a fit has been one of the harder and more valuable skills I have had to build.

## Living with unresolved uncertainty

The part nobody really prepares you for is how much of founding a company is just carrying open questions without an answer yet: will this client renew, is this hire the right one, is this feature worth building. There is rarely a clean resolution on any given day. The actual job, more than writing code or pitching clients, has been learning to function well while several important things stay unresolved at once.
