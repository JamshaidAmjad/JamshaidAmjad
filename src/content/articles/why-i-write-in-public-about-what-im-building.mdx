## Writing forces the thinking I skip otherwise

I write about Codexier AB, Forare, and what I am learning in my Master's because writing forces a kind of honesty that thinking alone does not. It is easy to believe a plan makes sense in my head and only notice the gaps once I try to explain it in a sentence someone else can read. Several decisions about Forare's scope only became clear to me after I tried, and failed, to explain them clearly in a draft.

## Public notes have done more than private polish

For a long time, I held off publishing anything until it felt finished or impressive. Nothing came from that approach except a folder of unpublished drafts. Once I started publishing earlier, including notes about things that were still half-working, real opportunities followed: better conversations with potential clients, people reaching out who were solving similar problems, and feedback that improved Codexier AB's direction faster than working in isolation did.

## Honesty is the only thing that makes it work

Writing in public only has value if it is actually honest. It would be easy to write a clean narrative about Codexier AB and Forare where everything went according to plan. That version would be useless to anyone reading it, including future me. So I try to write about what did not work, what I misjudged, and what I changed my mind about, even when it is less flattering than the polished version.

## What it has changed about how I build

Knowing I will eventually write about a decision changes how carefully I make it. It is a small, useful kind of accountability. I ask myself earlier whether a choice will make sense when I explain it later, which has quietly improved decisions in both the company and the Master's program before I ever publish a word about them.
